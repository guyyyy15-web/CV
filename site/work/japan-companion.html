Case study Own product

Japan Companion יפן בכיס

A pocket app for Hebrew speakers traveling in Japan. It does the two hardest things for a visitor: talking to people and handling money. Everything works without signal, from the subway to the mountains.

  • 15 versions in 9 days
  • 187 phrases with Hebrew pronunciation
  • 3,056 hand-checked builder sentences
  • ~490,000 places available offline

Open the app

The problem

Phrasebooks give you Japanese in romaji, which a Hebrew speaker mispronounces. Translation apps need signal and sometimes produce sentences no one would say. Prices are in yen, much of Japan still runs on cash, and the nearest public toilet is never where you are looking.

Product calls I made

  • Offline first. Phrases, signs, the converter and a snapshot of toilets, trash cans and convenience stores across Japan ship with the app. The map data is refreshed monthly by a scheduled job.
  • A builder, not free translation. You pick a situation, a sentence frame and a word, and only sensible combinations are offered: 58 frames and 3,056 combinations, each one checked. Free text is handed off to Google Translate.
  • Work with the iPhone, not against it. Speech recognition does not work in Home Screen apps, and Google blocks embedding Translate and Lens. So the app opens them one tap away instead of half-copying them.
  • Show it to staff. Any phrase opens as a full-screen card with the screen kept on, flipped to face the person you are talking to.
  • Ready for friends. The tab bar went from seven tabs to five, and a three-step intro greets anyone who opens the link for the first time.

Quality

80 unit tests cover the money maths, Hebrew and English string parity, the content schema and every builder sentence. A browser test on an iPhone-sized screen goes through every tab in both languages, offline, and on the small iPhone SE.

My role

Research, product plan, content rules, design and code, built with Claude Code. I wrote project skills for it (bilingual right-to-left UI, iPhone web app rules, Japanese content checks, the release check) so every change follows the same rules.
